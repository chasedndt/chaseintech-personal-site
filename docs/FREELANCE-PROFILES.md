## 0. Positioning — read this before writing anything

**Do not sell "full-stack developer".** That category has tens of thousands of
profiles competing on price, and you will lose to someone charging $12/hour.

Sell the thing almost nobody else can prove: **agentic AI systems that are safe
enough to actually deploy.** Every company experimenting with AI agents has the
same unspoken fear — that the agent will do something expensive, public and
irreversible. You have built the answer to that fear and can point at running
code: approval gates, permission ceilings, evidence-first review, a metering
architecture designed before the product.

That reframes the whole conversation. You are not competing on hourly rate
against generalists; you are one of a small number of people who have shipped
governance infrastructure for autonomous systems.

Three proof assets carry every profile:
1. **Chaser Agent** — MIT-licensed, public, review-first harness. Anyone can
   read the code.
2. **ChaseOS** — decision-modality routing, approval gates, live Forge
   marketplace at chaseos.ai.
3. **Build logs** — public engineering writing showing how decisions were made,
   including failures and corrections.

### Rate guidance (you decide — this is market context)
The cold-start problem is real: with no reviews, very high rates get filtered
out. But starting too low anchors you permanently and attracts the worst
clients.

- Opening rate while earning first reviews: **$50–70/hour**
- After ~3 strong reviews: **$85–120/hour**
- Specialist agentic/governance work, established: **$120–180/hour**

Never go below $40. Below that you attract clients who will consume more time
in scope disputes than the contract is worth, and Upwork's algorithm treats your
rate as a quality signal.

## 4. Toptal — do this last, or not yet

Toptal is **not a self-serve profile**. It's a multi-stage screening gauntlet:
English interview → timed algorithm test → technical deep-dive interview →
multi-day take-home project. Acceptance is reported around 3%, and the process
takes weeks.

Worth it eventually — Toptal rates are high and clients are serious. But go in
after you have reviews elsewhere, and expect to prepare specifically for the
timed algorithmic round, which tests something quite different from the systems
architecture you actually do.

**Recommended order: Upwork → Fiverr → Contra → (later) Toptal.**

## 6. The honest caveat

Platform work is a slow start: the first contract is by far the hardest, and
early proposals mostly go unanswered. The compounding asset is your own site and
public code — those keep working while you sleep. Treat the platforms as one
channel, not the strategy.
